## Mon, Nov 23

**Top pick:** Google is funding AI literacy in Canada (over $7M CAD from Google.org to more than a dozen non-profits, aiming to reach 550,000+ youth and educators) in the same week more than 540 of its own employees and contractors asked it to let schools keep Search without the AI answers. That's the tension schools are living in, and a strong, non-partisan Growthway story.

### 1. Google funds Canadian AI literacy as its own workers push back on AI-or-no-Search for schools · score 9/10
Google Canada says Google.org is providing "over $7M CAD in funding... to over a dozen Canadian non-profit organizations," which "will reach over 550,000 Canadian youth and educators." The AI literacy share includes Actua and Digital Moment working with the Raspberry Pi Foundation on Experience AI, Community AI Literacy Grants (Pathways to Education, Amii, Simon Fraser University, the Society for Arts and Technology, and public library foundations in Calgary and Vancouver), and MediaSmarts. Separately, a petition organized by the Alphabet Workers Union and signed by "more than 540 Google employees and contractors," public Nov. 19, asks Google to let schools "keep Google Search, but turn off the AI answers." · [Google Canada](https://blog.google/intl/en-ca/company-news/outreach-initiatives/empowering-canadian-youth-families-and-educators/) · [iPhone in Canada, citing Bloomberg](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2026/11/20/google-workers-school-search-without-ai/)
**Why it matters:** The funding is good news for programs schools already use. But AI literacy for 550,000 people doesn't settle the question in front of every board: what to switch on before Google's default changes. And even Google's staff think "AI or no Search" is the wrong choice to force.

_The petition is reported secondhand (Bloomberg via iPhone in Canada); I didn't open Bloomberg._

### 2. Labour Board rules on Ontario bargaining; minister: "absolutely no imminent strike threat" · score 8/10
All five Ontario education unions now hold strike mandates, "ranging from 94 to 98 per cent." On Nov. 18 the Ontario Labour Relations Board "issued a decision clarifying which issues can be negotiated centrally and which must be addressed locally, clearing the way for substantive bargaining to resume." Education Minister Paul Calandra: "There's absolutely no imminent strike threat because there's a number of processes that have to be put in place before that can even start." OECTA's president said job action before Christmas would be "highly unlikely." A legal strike requires conciliation and a no-board report, then a 17-day countdown and at least five days' notice. · [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/11/20/all-five-of-ontarios-education-unions-now-have-strike-mandates-here-is-what-that-could-mean-for-students/)
**Why it matters:** This is the explainer parents need: talks are finally moving, and several legal steps stand between a mandate and any job action. Growthway can share the process without taking a side.
