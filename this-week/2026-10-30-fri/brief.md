### 1. Eskasoni School Board drops facial scanning of teachers after union concerns · score 9/10
Wall-mounted tablets installed in the spring showed each staff member's photo, full name and recent attendance as they scanned in and out. The Eskasoni Teachers' Union says members had "serious concerns about where their biometric data was being stored" and didn't believe they had given informed consent; staff learned on the first day of this school year that the program was ending. "We're very happy that our voice was heard," said union president Jonathan Cox. The board, which runs six schools, didn't respond to CBC. · [coverage (CBC via Yahoo)](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/eskasoni-school-board-drops-facial-182156062.html)
**Why it matters:** Biometric and AI tools are showing up in schools for attendance and security. Boards need to ask before they buy: where is the data stored, who consented, and how long is it kept?

### 2. Alberta's Bill 35: provincial report card standards and public PD-day reports · score 8/10
Bill 35 would let the education minister set provincial report card standards (boards keep formats), require school authorities to publicly disclose what happened on PD and other non-instructional days within 60 days, and require boards to set up processes for parent concerns not resolved at school. It also adds ministerial approval before a board renames a school. Changes take effect on proclamation, expected for 2027–28. · [primary source](https://www.alberta.ca/announcements.cfm?xID=98032558BC0F4-B1DE-6BF0-C6F1957D67CDFA41)
**Why it matters:** More information for parents, but also more reporting for schools. Whether it helps depends on what the standards say, and the Ministerial Orders aren't written yet.

### 3. Alberta bill would cover AI-generated intimate images, a week after the Lévis deepfakes · score 8/10
Bill 33 (Oct. 27) would expand Alberta's intimate-images law to "digitally altered and AI-generated intimate images, videos and audio," create a provincial offence with fines up to $10,000, up to two years in jail, or both, and let victims sue even when content is only threatened, not shared. This follows this week's story from Lévis, Que., where students were suspended for AI-altered images of staff (police and the board deny any were sexual). · [primary source](https://www.alberta.ca/announcements.cfm?xID=98021F6651386-B162-68FB-0F3314F80DCA0DEB)
**Why it matters:** The law is starting to catch up with deepfakes. Students need to hear it in class before they meet it in court.
